This policy describes how AI Influencer Studio handles information in its public informational website and private development workspace. For privacy or deletion requests, contact {{CONTACT}}.

1. Information handled by the application

  • Public website: These informational pages do not include account signup, contact forms, analytics scripts, advertising trackers, or credential collection. The hosting service may process request information such as IP address, requested page, browser information, and timestamp to serve and secure the website.
  • Persona information: The private workspace stores creator-supplied names, traits, interests, goals, fictional biographies, saved Creator chat messages, generated drafts, activity timestamps, and public profile links. Identity & Debug test messages remain in browser component memory and are not saved by the studio; they are sent to the configured inference server when a test reply is requested.
  • Authorized accounts: When you authorize a provider, the studio stores the account’s provider, identifier, available display name or email address, granted scopes, token expiry, and access/refresh tokens returned by the provider. The precise information depends on the service and the permission granted.
  • Optional vault records: The vault stores credentials you explicitly enter, including usernames and passwords, along with service labels, website links, and optional persona assignments. Provider OAuth passwords are entered on provider websites, not collected by the studio’s OAuth flow.

2. Why information is used

Information is used to manage personas, maintain character context, respond to creator chats, generate unpublished drafts, display account connections, renew Google access tokens, and manage saved credentials. Basic Google identity authorization does not read Gmail, Drive, or YouTube data. TikTok and Snapchat identity linking does not enable publishing or private-message access. Social posting is not implemented in this development version.

3. AI inference and third-party services

When generation is requested, the persona’s profile, biography, recent relevant chat context, and the creator’s request are sent to the configured inference server. The default setup uses a locally operated llama.cpp server; an operator may configure a hosted provider instead. The chosen provider’s own terms and data practices apply to that processing.

Saved vault passwords, OAuth tokens, and administrator keys are not included in AI prompts or used by this application to train models. OAuth requests and Google token renewals are sent to the relevant provider to perform the authorization functions you requested. Hosting providers process public website traffic to deliver the site. The application does not implement sale of account data or advertising data sharing.

4. Storage and security

Workspace data is stored in the backend’s SQLite database. Saved usernames/passwords and provider tokens are encrypted before storage using a backend-managed key. Labels, fictional biography data, chats, drafts, account identifiers, and other metadata are not encrypted by the application’s database layer. The operator must protect the server, encryption key, database, and backups and use HTTPS for remote access.

The studio is a single-user development application with administrator-key protection for credential-management functions, not a fully reviewed multi-user security system. No storage or transmission method provides an absolute security guarantee.

5. Browser storage and authorization cookies

The private dashboard saves your theme preference in browser local storage. Its administrator key is held in browser memory rather than persistent browser storage. OAuth uses short-lived, browser-bound HttpOnly state cookies that expire after ten minutes and are removed when a callback completes. These public informational pages do not set application cookies or browser-storage values.

6. Retention and deletion

Workspace records remain until the operator deletes them. You can disconnect authorized accounts or delete saved vault records in the dashboard, or ask the operator to remove information relating to you. Disconnecting removes locally stored tokens; revoke the application in the provider’s security settings to remove the provider-side authorization too.

Deleting a persona removes its profile, biography, chats, drafts, saved public links, and connection tokens. Separately saved vault credentials are retained as unassigned workspace accounts to avoid destroying access to an external account. Deleting a saved credential does not delete the account at the external provider. Backups and hosting logs follow the operator’s deployment and retention settings; deletion from the active database does not instantly erase older backups.

7. Your choices and requests

Choose which accounts to connect and which information to save. You may revoke provider permissions, disconnect accounts, and request access, correction, or deletion of information associated with you. Contact {{CONTACT}} with enough non-sensitive context to identify the relevant record. The operator may need to verify that you are authorized to request the change. Do not send passwords, administrator keys, access tokens, or encryption keys.

8. Policy updates

The operator may revise this policy when features or data handling change. The last-updated date above identifies this version. Changes that add Gmail access, publishing, new data processing, or new providers should be reflected in this policy before those features are made available.
